0. About this manual

This manual explains how to run a small secured-lending business (a shop that lends money against an item the borrower leaves as security) with the Localised Micro Financing System. The worked example throughout is a gold pawn / sale-with-right-of-redemption shop (ขายฝาก), because that is the business the system was shaped around. Nothing in the method is specific to gold: other collateral (watches, phones, vehicles’ papers, equipment) follows the same steps with different categories and rate tables. Where a rule comes from Thai law or practice it is marked [TH]; a shop elsewhere replaces those settings.

Status of this edition. The screens, rules and records described here all run, on sample data, in the demonstration application. Some outside connections are stand-ins and are called out where they appear: [stub] = a stand-in with the same shape as the real thing. Section 13 lists exactly what is real and what is a stand-in. Do not use this edition to run real money.

How to read it. Sections 1–3 give the ideas and the first-day setup. Sections 4–8 are the daily routine, in the order a day goes. Section 9 is a screen-by-screen reference. Sections 10–12 cover the rules, record-keeping and safety, and the daily checks. Section 13 says what is real and what is a stand-in, section 14 shows how to adapt the system to another kind of lending, and section 15 is troubleshooting.

1. What the system does

You want to… The system…
Lend against an item and keep the item safe Issues a ticket (the loan contract), records the item, its weight/value, and where it is stored (warehouse, aisle, bin, level, lot)
Charge interest correctly and legally Computes interest by your rules, caps it at the legal ceiling, adds tax when your setting says so
Let the borrower see and pay from a phone Gives the borrower a sealed offer (QR/link + one-time code), then payment by bank-transfer QR with slip checking
Know what is happening today A dashboard that gives the big picture at a glance: loan lifecycle, risk, price cushion, vault map, quota, data safety
Follow up late payers Overdue list, reminders with the borrower’s own amounts, a contact history
Close the day A cash count and vault count against what the records say should be there
Never lose or quietly change a record Every event is signed and chained; changes are detected; backups are encrypted; an optional black-box journal keeps a second copy
Work with several staff and counters Roles (owner, cashier, viewer) and several counters on one ledger

The system keeps one ledger of events. Every figure you see — a balance, a status, a total — is calculated from those events every time you look. There are no hidden running totals that can drift out of step. That is why a restored backup shows exactly the same numbers as before.

2. The ideas you need

Ticket. One loan: borrower, collateral, amount, rate, term, due date. It has a number such as A161. The letter is the counter’s prefix.

Principal. The money lent and still owed. Interest is charged on the principal outstanding. Paying interest does not reduce principal; paying principal does.

Status. A ticket moves through: Offered (shop issued, borrower has not acknowledged) → Active (acknowledged, in term) → Overdue (past the due date) → Redeemed (paid in full, collateral returned) or Forfeited (collateral kept by the shop) → Sold (forfeited item sold).

Closed tickets never change. A top-up, a part-repayment or a renewal does not edit the ticket. The old ticket is closed and a new ticket is issued that refers to it. Only the difference in money moves. This is the single most important habit of the system: you never “fix” a record, you add a correcting one.

Collateral and location. The item is described once (category, weight, bag/pack number) and has a lot (one per ticket chain, so a renewal keeps the same lot) and a locator (aisle–bin–level, e.g. A-03-2).

Signed record. When something happens (a ticket is issued, a payment is booked, an item is moved) the system writes an event, signs it, and chains it to the one before. If anyone later changes an old event, the signature and chain no longer match and the system shows it in red.

Chooser (setting). Where shops legitimately differ (how tax is charged, how part-periods round, the late-loan threshold) the system offers a choice instead of forcing one answer. Choosers are listed in section 10.

Local-first: the borrower carries the whole history. The borrower’s phone holds a complete, signed copy of their own ticket: the offer, the schedule, the rate they agreed, every payment and receipt. Everything that can be worked out from that history — what is due and when, the interest owed today, what it would cost to redeem on any day, whether a receipt is genuine — the phone works out by itself, with no connection at all. So the phone can remind its owner against the timeline, show balances, and prepare a payment, entirely offline. It only needs to talk to the shop for things the phone cannot know on its own (section 6.5). The shop is local-first in the same way: its dashboard, books and counter work with no phone connected.

The three parties. The shop (counter and back office), the borrower (phone), and the ledger they share. Each side keeps its own copy and they exchange small signed messages (“tokens”), so the system keeps working when one side is offline.

3. Before the first loan — setup checklist

Do these once, in this order. Each has a screen in the Admin group unless noted.

  1. Language. The button at the top right switches Thai / English at any time.
  2. Settings. Pick your profile (Case 1: monthly interest, normal shop; Case 2: daily interest, company with a borrower app). Check the tax type and when tax is charged, the rounding of part-periods, the lending limit mode, and the late-loan threshold. Check the two statutory limits (customer identification and cash-report amounts): you may set them stricter, never looser [TH].
  3. Gold price (Gold and collateral group). Record today’s price, with date. Appraisals use it. Record it every day; a price older than a day triggers a warning.
  4. Staff and counters. Add your staff and give each a PIN. The first time you add someone you set the owner PIN. Set this counter’s ID and ticket prefix (for example C1 / A). With no staff defined, you work alone as the owner and nothing is restricted.
  5. Theme. Optional: colours, corner radius and text size so the screens match your shop.
  6. Existing loans. If you already have live tickets, use Import existing tickets (section 9.7) to bring them in as opening balances.
  7. Backup. Create your recovery code, write it down, and save a first backup (section 11.2). Do this before the first real day.

Write the recovery code on paper and keep it somewhere safe. It is shown once and is never stored. Without it a backup cannot be opened.

4. Opening the shop — the daily start

  1. Open the Dashboard. Look at the data-safety tile: when was the last backup, and is the journal up to date?
  2. Record today’s gold price (Gold price). The dashboard shows the change and the cushion (value of collateral minus the money lent out).
  3. Open Due-date reminders. It lists tickets due soon or overdue. Prepare and send the messages (section 7.2).
  4. If you keep a cash drawer, the Day-end screen shows the opening float: yesterday’s counted cash.

5. Issuing a loan

Use Counter → Issue ticket, then hand over in Loan hand-over.

5.1 Fill in the ticket

  1. Customer: name, phone (the phone number is the borrower’s identity in the system), rate group.
  2. Collateral: category, item, quantity, weight, gold type (the percentage the shop lends), the price basis, bag/pack number.
  3. The screen shows market value, the ceiling (the most you may lend), your headroom, and the loan-to-value ratio, which sets the longest term.
  4. Enter the loan amount, never above the ceiling. Interest runs on what is drawn, not on the ceiling.
  5. Terms: date, months, rate (from the table), and the agreed interest per month. You may agree a lower figure (a discount). The system refuses anything above the legal ceiling and uses the maximum instead [TH 15% a year].
  6. Statutory limits panel:
    • Customer identification: from a set amount the ID number and a copy are required before issue. The ID number is kept inside your shop only, never sent to the borrower.
    • Term limit: a redemption right may not run more than 3 years from the first sale [TH]. A term over the limit is cut to the maximum.
    • Cash report: a cash payout at or above the threshold is flagged on the dashboard so you can make the required report yourself.
  7. Choose how you pay out: cash or bank transfer.

5.2 Issue

Press Issue. A ticket number is assigned, the loan is recorded, and a free locator is suggested by loan value so the item has a place from the start. The screen then takes you to Loan hand-over.

If you are signed in as a viewer, or as a cashier without the right, the action is refused, a red banner explains why, and nothing is written.

6. Handing over to the borrower, and taking payments

6.1 Hand-over (Loan hand-over)

The offer is sealed: it can only be opened with a one-time code.

  1. The screen shows a QR and a link for the offer, and the code.
  2. Give the code to the borrower by another route (a text message, or say it). The QR/link alone opens nothing.
  3. The borrower scans the QR or opens the link, enters the code, reads the terms (loan, rate, due date, schedule), and presses Acknowledge.
  4. The acknowledgement comes back to the shop by any channel (web, QR, WhatsApp, LINE, SMS, e-mail, NFC, pasted). When it arrives the ticket becomes Active.

The wire panel shows every message with a padlock, so you can see that nothing travelled in the clear. If a message is lost or arrives twice or out of order, the next message repairs it and duplicates are ignored. If the borrower is offline, the acknowledgement waits and is sent when they are back.

6.2 Taking a payment

The borrower can choose:

Choice Meaning
Installment The next scheduled interest payment (plus tax if your setting adds it)
Pay in full Redeem: principal + interest owed + tax
Another amount Applied to interest and tax first; any rest reduces the principal (see 6.4)

Payment is by bank-transfer QR. The borrower then sends the bank slip. The shop’s system checks the slip with the slip-check service [stub in this edition]. A good check books the payment, attaches the service’s reply to the record as evidence, and sends the borrower a signed receipt. A duplicate slip, a wrong amount, a wrong receiving account, or an unreadable slip is refused with the reason shown to the borrower. If the bank or the service is down, the payment waits and is checked again; the borrower is not blamed.

Money that was really paid is never refused because the quote had gone stale: the amount on the slip is applied to interest first, as “another amount”.

6.3 Cash at the counter

A payment taken in cash is entered at the counter. It is marked cash and appears in the day-end count. (Bank-transfer payments never appear in the cash drawer.)

6.4 Top-up, part-repayment, renewal — one mechanism

For every change to an open loan the system does the same three things:

  1. The old ticket is closed by a non-cash receipt (no principal moves in cash).
  2. A new ticket is issued, referring to the old one, with the new principal.
  3. Only the difference is paid: new principal − old principal − interest owed. If positive the shop pays the customer; if negative the customer pays the shop.

A top-up can go as high as the ceiling at today’s gold price. The shop keeps its percentage as a cushion against a falling price.

6.5 When the phone and the shop need to talk — and when they don’t

Two-way communication is not a constant background link. The phone works alone; it talks to the shop only for the cases below. Everything travels as small signed messages over whatever pipe is available — the shop’s web server, a QR, a link, WhatsApp, LINE, SMS, e-mail, an NFC tap, or a pasted text. It can arrive late, twice or out of order; the signatures and the chain sort it out.

What the phone does alone (no communication at all)

Scenario Why no contact is needed
Looking at the ticket: terms, schedule, due date, payment history It is all in the phone’s own copy
Reminders before each due date Calculated from the schedule against the phone’s clock
“How much is owed today?” and “what will redeem cost on the 20th?” Principal, paid-to date and the rate fixed in the signed offer are all on the phone
Checking a receipt is genuine The receipt is signed by the shop’s key; the phone verifies it itself
Checking the history has not been changed The chain is verified locally
Preparing a payment The phone builds the bank-transfer QR with the amount from the shop’s account details in the offer
Starting a claim while offline The claim (with the slip) is queued and sent when a connection returns

What needs a message, and which way

Scenario Direction Why How urgent
Acknowledging an offer phone → shop The shop must hear that the borrower accepted before the ticket is Active Soon; waits offline
Claiming a payment (slip) phone → shop Only the shop can check the slip and book the payment Soon after paying
The shop’s answer: receipt, or rejected with the reason shop → phone Only the shop knows the check result Soon after paying
A payment taken in cash at the counter, a discount, a correction shop → phone The phone cannot know what happened at the counter; until it hears, its figures are out of date When the borrower next looks
Today’s gold price, and room left to borrow more shop → phone The price lives with the shop and changes daily Only when the borrower wants a top-up
A changed rate or new terms (for a new ticket or a top-up) shop → phone An existing ticket keeps its signed rate; only a new ticket gets new terms Only for new tickets
Renewal, top-up, reduction (a new linked ticket) both ways A new document must be issued and acknowledged When the borrower asks
Forfeit or sale shop → phone A shop-side event the phone cannot foresee At the time
Phone lost: new device shop → new phone A new code and the history must be delivered At the time
Offers and news shop → phone Only the shop has them; sent only to people who agreed Optional

The “Check for latest” idea. Where the phone cannot know, it shows what it knows and says how old that is (“as of your last contact, 12 Sep”) and offers a Check for latest button. Pressing it does not download “the system”: the phone says which events it has already seen, and the shop sends back only the missing signed events (a few small pieces of JSON), through the same pipe the two have been using, or any other. A tiny signed status token (just “I have up to this event, as of this time”) lets either side see at a glance whether the other is in sync, behind, or ahead. If there is nothing new, the shop’s reply says so, signed, so the phone can show “up to date as of ”. If the pipe is not available, nothing is lost: the figures stay on screen, marked with their age, and the check can be done later.

Why stale figures are not dangerous. If the borrower pays from an out-of-date figure (for example, they did not know a cash payment had been taken yesterday), the shop applies the money to interest first and the difference stays as a credit or reduces principal; the shop never refuses money that was really paid because a quote was old. The shop’s own books are always the authority, and the phone’s copy is corrected by the next message.

Which scenarios need the shop’s reply to be quick? Only paying and being told the result. Everything else can wait for the next time the borrower looks or the next time there is a connection, which is why the system works with a weak or intermittent signal.

The demonstration shows the behind / in sync indicator on the borrower’s phone, queues messages while the phone is “offline”, delivers them when it is “online”, and repairs a lost or repeated message with the next one. A separate Check for latest button and the “up to date as of” mark are the intended design, not yet a screen of their own.

7. When payments are late

7.1 Watching risk

The dashboard classes each open ticket automatically:

  • Green list — not late, at least one installment paid, room left under the ceiling. These are good customers you may offer a top-up.
  • Watch — overdue, or late on an installment.
  • NPL (non-performing) — overdue by more than your threshold (default 90 days).

Click any figure to see the tickets behind it; click a ticket to see its history, with a mark beside every event showing whether it still verifies.

7.2 Reminders and follow-up

The shop does not nag on-time borrowers; the phone reminds its own owner. Because the borrower has the whole history (section 2), the phone knows every due date and amount and can remind against the timeline even offline: a banner each time the app is opened (“installment due in 3 days · interest owed today · cost to redeem”), and an optional calendar entry for each due date that works in any calendar program without a connection. The shop sends no reminder to someone paying on time; this also avoids message costs and the need for permission to message people about their own loan. The shop chases the late. Borrower-side reminders are the rule of the system but are not built in this edition: the demonstration phone already shows due date and interest owed from its own copy; banners and calendar entries are not yet there. The shop-side screen below is what the demonstration has.

Due-date reminders (shop side) is therefore for three uses: borrowers who have no phone app or are not enrolled (counter-only tickets, imported opening balances), late tickets, and offers to good customers. It lists open tickets due within a number of days you set, or already overdue.

  • A message is prepared per person, in Thai or English, with that ticket’s own numbers: interest owed, the amount to redeem, the next installment, and the statement that the rate is within the legal limit.
  • Only people who have agreed to receive messages are included. If a borrower has not agreed, press Record consent when they agree at the counter.
  • You choose the channel (WhatsApp, SMS, LINE, e-mail). The system opens the messaging application with the text ready; you press send. Each prepared message is written into the ticket’s follow-up history (date, channel, outcome, next date). Follow-up notes stay inside the shop; they are never sent to the borrower.

7.3 Forfeiting and selling

When a ticket is past due and not redeemed, the shop may forfeit it (Forfeit and sale).

  1. Select the overdue ticket. The system proposes a re-appraisal at today’s buy price; you may change it.
  2. The screen shows the gain or loss against the principal and the unpaid interest that is written off.
  3. Press Forfeit. The item moves to the forfeited stock locator and the shop owns it at the appraised value.
  4. Later, Sell: enter price, buyer and how paid. The item leaves stock; the system shows gain or loss against the appraised value.

You cannot forfeit a ticket that is not yet due, or already redeemed, or already forfeited, and you can only sell forfeited items. Tax on the sale of forfeited gold is set by your accountant; the system does not guess it.

8. Redeeming — closing a loan well

  1. Open Counter → Redeem for the ticket.
  2. Check the figures: loan, interest owed to date, discount if any, tax base, tax, total. The tax base is only the interest collected in this transaction: never the gold, never principal [TH].
  3. Take the money: cash and transfer amounts must add up to the total.
  4. Press Show receipt / tax invoice and then Print. The receipt lists each ticket (weight, redemption value, pawn value, tax base, tax), the total received, the cash/transfer split, and the signature lines. A receipt whose cash and transfer do not add up to the amount received is refused.
  5. Return the item. In Collateral locator, take it out; the vault count falls by one.

9. Screen-by-screen reference

Menu groups on a computer are down the left; on a phone the bottom bar holds the four daily tabs and a More sheet holds the rest.

9.1 Daily

Counter. The staff screens, chosen from a list of five actions: Issue ticket, Pay interest, Top-up / reduce, Redeem, Forfeit. They show the full arithmetic on the screen and a printed-ticket preview. (The first version of the demonstration, at /m/, also has a Borrower app tab showing what the borrower sees; in the current version the borrower side is in Loan hand-over.)

Loan hand-over. The shop panel, the message wire and the borrower’s phone side by side. Use it to issue offers, see the code, send by any channel, simulate offline/online, and, if a phone is lost, revoke and re-enrol (section 11.5).

Dashboard. The big picture. Top row: Loan lifecycle (counts by status), Open principal by risk (donut), NPL ratio (gauge), Slip-check quota (meter). Next: Vault map, Cash collected per month, Data safety, Gold price, Price cushion. Then tiles and lists (open loans, principal, interest owed, overdue, NPL, green list, items not located, cash to report, due soon). Click any element to drill down. Customise lets you drag widgets, resize them, hide them, add one from a form, or edit the layout as text; the layout is saved for your shop.

Due-date reminders. Section 7.2.

Day-end. Pick the day. The screen lists the day’s cash movements (payouts out, counter receipts in, cash sales), shows expected closing cash = opening float + net movement, and the number of bank transfers that are not counted as cash. Enter the cash counted; the variance = counted − expected shows in red if not zero (a negative number means cash is short). Add a note and Save: a signed record is kept in the history. Tomorrow’s opening float is today’s counted cash. The vault count from Collateral locator is saved alongside.

9.2 Gold and collateral

Collateral locator. The vault map (aisles down, bins across, number = bags). Tap a cell to list what is there. Put away / move: choose the ticket, the locator and the bag number. The Not located list must be empty (it flags any open ticket without a place). Day-end count: expected per locator against what you counted. Movements: the log of put-aways, moves, take-outs and forfeits.

Forfeit and sale. Section 7.3.

Gold price. Record a dated price: buy-back, sell, tax-base. Each ticket keeps a copy of the price it was issued at, so a later price change never alters an issued ticket. The cushion panel shows collateral value at today’s buy price against principal out, and counts tickets where principal now exceeds the gold value.

9.3 Money

Slip-check usage. Slips checked this month against your plan’s quota, how many were over quota, estimated cost, cost per verified payment, share rejected, and a six-month chart. It warns at 80% and 100% of quota. It is an estimate: your provider’s invoice is the authority.

Books (GL). The accounting entries each event would post, a trial balance, an income statement (interest, gain/loss) and a tax summary, all calculated from the same events. This edition shows a preview; it does not post to a general ledger.

9.4 Customers

Contacts. Import customer lists from Excel, CSV, a pasted table, a phone-contacts file, a Google Contacts file, the phone’s contact picker, or LINE ids. The system normalises Thai phone numbers, removes duplicates (phone, then e-mail, then LINE id, filling blanks and never overwriting), and shows a review before anything is added. Each contact has consent; offers and news go only to consented contacts. Choose an audience (green-list customers, all with a ticket, contacts without a ticket, everyone who agreed), write or use the offer text, pick the channel, and the system prepares one message per person; you press send. Every import and broadcast is recorded.

Import existing tickets. Section 9.7 below.

9.5 Admin

Staff and counters. Staff, roles and the who-can-do-what table; sign in by name and PIN; counter ID and ticket prefix; Sync with the other counters; the conflicts list.

Settings. The choosers (section 10) and the legal limits.

Theme. Colour tokens, corner radius, font size, and ready-made looks. The change is shown live on the borrower phone and the dashboard.

Data model (AD). The data dictionary: every table and column the system uses, with types, lists and which screens use them. The forms, columns and group-by choices on other screens are generated from this dictionary, so hiding a column here removes it everywhere.

Fit for Thailand. What is already in line with Thai practice and what is not yet. Offline, no cloud. How the system works without a server, and its limits. Film chapters. A guided walk-through of the whole system.

9.7 Import existing tickets (opening balances)

For a shop that already has live loans.

  1. Download the template; fill one row per open ticket: ticket number, date, name, phone, loan, months, rate (or group), paid-to date, category, weight, locator.
  2. Choose the file (Excel or CSV) or paste the table.
  3. Review: rows read, rows valid, rows invalid with the reason (missing number, bad date, future date, repeated number, paid-to outside the loan’s life, months outside 1–36, unknown category), the principal that would be imported, tickets needing identification, rates cut to the legal ceiling.
  4. Import. One signed record per ticket is made; no payment history is invented — only the principal and the date interest is paid to. Importing the same file again adds nothing.
  5. The imported tickets appear in Not located until you put them away.

10. The rules the system applies

10.1 Interest

  • Rate is looked up from your table by rate group and loan band; the system never applies more than the legal ceiling [TH: 15% a year, 1.25% a month].
  • Agreed interest defaults to the table rate and may be set lower (a discount, shown).
  • Part-periods (paying part-way through a period) follow your chooser: month, half-month (15 days), week, or pro-rata by day.
  • Case 1 charges by month; Case 2 charges by day (rate × 12 ÷ 365).
  • Interest owed to date is always calculated from the paid-to date, never stored.

10.2 Tax on interest [TH: VAT 7%]

  • Tax is on interest only; the item and the principal are not taxed.
  • When it is charged is a chooser: at every interest payment (default, in line with the tax authority’s gold-shop guidance), only at ticket close, or once at final redeem (flagged: not in line with the guidance, for your accountant).
  • It may be shown on top (exclusive) or inside (inclusive).
  • A discount on the interest lowers the tax base.

10.3 Lending limit

Either a percentage of the appraised value by gold type (for example 87 / 84 / 82%), or a fixed amount deducted per baht-weight of gold. The loan may not exceed the ceiling. The loan-to-value ratio sets the longest term (for example up to 80% → 3 months).

10.4 Statutory limits [TH]

Limit What the system does
Interest ceiling 15%/year Refuses a higher rate, uses the maximum
Redemption right ≤ 3 years from the first sale Cuts a term to the maximum; blocks a renewal that would pass it
Customer identification from a set amount Requires the ID number and a copy before issue
Cash report from a set amount Flags a cash payout at or above the threshold

The two amounts are settings; your shop may set them lower. Re-check the current rules before using them in a real business.

11. Keeping the records safe

11.1 What “signed and chained” gives you

Every event carries a signature and a link to the one before. If an old event is edited, deleted or re-ordered, the history view shows ✖ in red at that event and at everything after it. You can check any ticket’s history at any time. This proves nothing was altered; it does not prove that what was originally entered was true.

11.2 Backup

  1. Recovery code: press Create a new recovery code. Write it down. It encrypts backups and opens them.
  2. Save to a file: an encrypted .gpb file. Keep copies off the machine.
  3. Send to a web address: the encrypted file goes to a server you control. The server holds only data it cannot read.
  4. Share / e-mail: on a phone, the share sheet; on a computer, the file is saved and your mail program opens with a summary — attach the file yourself. A browser cannot send mail on its own.
  5. A banner reminds you when the last backup is old or many records are new.

11.3 Restore

Choose the file and type the recovery code. The system opens it, checks every ticket’s chain, and shows a summary (what is new, same, or conflicting) before changing anything. A ticket that fails the check is not restored. Newer data on this device is never overwritten. If the code is wrong or the file was altered, nothing opens and nothing is changed.

11.4 The automatic journal (“black box”)

Turn it on and every new record is encrypted and sent, a moment after you stop working, to the journal server. The server can only add to its record, cannot edit or delete, and cannot read the content. You see how many records are sent and not yet sent. Audit against the server checks that nothing was dropped or changed and shows the result. After a disaster, Restore from the journal on a new device, with your recovery code, rebuilds everything up to the last record sent. Records not yet sent when the device is lost are lost — that is why the “not yet sent” count is on the dashboard.

11.5 Sealed messages and the borrower’s phone

  • Messages between shop and borrower are encrypted to each other; a link or QR passed through any channel reveals nothing.
  • The first offer opens only with the one-time code; a wrong code opens nothing and is forgotten.
  • The borrower’s phone keys are bound to that device and cannot be copied out.
  • If the borrower’s phone is lost: on the ticket in Loan hand-over press Phone lost: revoke the key and re-enrol a new device. The shop gets a new code and a link; the new device opens it with the new code. The old phone can no longer sign anything new; the history stays and still checks.

11.6 Roles

Owner Cashier Viewer
Issue, take payment, redeem, put away, count, record price, day-end, reminders, follow-up ✔ ✔ –
Forfeit, sell, import tickets, change settings, backup/restore, revoke a phone, manage staff and counters ✔ – –

Who acted, and in which role, is written into every signed event. The PIN is a convenience gate in a static demonstration page, not real security. A shop needs a server or a key per person for real enforcement.

11.7 Several counters

Counters share one ledger through the journal. Each has its own ticket prefix. Sync first sends this counter’s records, then pulls the others and merges them. If counter A and counter B changed the same ticket at the same point, the system does not merge it: it keeps this counter’s version and lists the conflict with both versions’ fingerprints, so a person decides. Re-enter what differs.

12. What should always be true — your daily checks

A well-run shop can check these at any time. The system is built so that each one holds.

  1. Every event in a ticket’s history shows ✔.
  2. Vault count = open tickets; Not located = 0.
  3. Principal out (dashboard) = the receivable in the books preview.
  4. The trial balance balances (debits = credits).
  5. Cash counted at day-end equals expected, or the variance is written down with a reason. Never adjusted away.
  6. Tax collected equals the tax on the tax invoices for the period.
  7. No payment on a closed ticket; no interest above the legal ceiling; no negative principal.
  8. A backup is recent, and the journal shows 0 not yet sent or a small, explained number.
  9. After a restore, the same numbers appear as before.

14. Adapting the system to another kind of lending

The same flow fits other secured loans. What you change is data, not method.

  1. Collateral categories and items. Replace the list (for example phone, watch, motorbike papers). Keep weight/size, description, serial number.
  2. Valuation. Replace “weight ÷ 15.16 × price” by your own appraisal source (a price list, a quoted value). The ceiling is appraised value × the percentage you lend.
  3. Rate table. Rate by group and loan band, effective dates, and a promotion row if you run one. Always keep your jurisdiction’s ceiling in place.
  4. Term rules. Default term, the ratio-to-term table, the longest redemption period your law allows.
  5. Tax. Whether interest is taxed, when, and whether shown inside or on top. Remove it if not applicable.
  6. Locations. Warehouses and locators (safe, shelf, bin) match your premises; forfeited stock may be a different location from customer collateral.
  7. Forfeit and sale. Replace with your repossession rules (notice periods, auction) as additional steps before forfeit.
  8. Identification and reporting limits. Enter your jurisdiction’s figures.
  9. Language and wording. Add a language; notices on tickets, receipts and messages follow your wording.
  10. What stays the same: one signed ledger; closed tickets never change; every figure calculated from events; backups encrypted; roles; the daily checks in section 12.

15. Troubleshooting

Problem What to do
A red banner says an action is not allowed for the role Sign in as a person with the right, or ask the owner. Nothing was written.
A ticket history shows ✖ An event was changed or is missing. Restore the ticket from a backup or the journal; do not edit.
The borrower cannot open the offer Check they typed the one-time code you gave them, not the ticket number. A wrong code opens nothing.
Payment stays “waiting” The bank or slip service may be down; use check again. Quota problems show on the Slip-check usage screen.
Slip rejected as duplicate That slip was already used. Ask for the new transfer slip.
A ticket is Not located Open Collateral locator, choose it, choose a locator, save.
Cash at day-end is short Do not change the expected figure. Record the variance and a note; investigate the day’s cash movements listed on the screen.
A restore says “cannot open” Wrong recovery code or an altered file. Try the code again exactly as written; use another copy.
Sync lists a conflict Two counters changed the same ticket at once. Keep this counter’s version, check what the other counter recorded, and re-enter it by hand.
The new price is not used on an issued ticket By design: a ticket keeps the price it was issued at.
An imported file is refused row by row Read the reason column; correct the file and import again. Rows already imported are skipped.
You lost the recovery code Backups made with it cannot be opened. Create a new code and make a new backup; this is why the code is written on paper.
